    VMS competitive improvement plan¶

    Prepared 13 October 2026, Asia/Calcutta. Status: proposed engineering and customer-validation plan; not an approved delivery schedule or release claim.

    Outcome¶

    Make Sentinel Grid the customer's preferred VMS by improving the daily jobs they perform: watching cameras, finding footage, exporting evidence, identifying failed devices, and handling incidents. Cover every major buying criterion, but deliver changes in order of customer impact and prove each improvement.

    The customer named VC Tech's VMS but gave no specific reason for preferring it. VC Tech Smart VMS at VC Tech AI is the working comparison; the exact installed product version, configuration, pricing, and customer experience have not been supplied. Public descriptions are vendor claims, not independently measured performance.

    Competitor baseline¶

    VC Tech advertises single-to-64-camera grids, timeline playback at 1x/2x/4x, screenshots, automatic recording replenishment after network outages, maker-checker access, hierarchical permissions, and web/Android/iOS access. Adjacent offerings include device and recording health, scheduled reports, industry-specific analytics, cloud/hybrid storage, human monitoring, and maintenance ticketing. Source: official product descriptions, reviewed 13 October 2026.

    Compare the licensed VMS package first. Price optional analytics, cloud storage, monitoring staff, and maintenance separately on both sides. A competitor's managed service cannot be matched through interface changes alone.

    Current findings that change the order of work¶

    Mobile route behavior needs correction first¶

    src/routes/mobile-operations.routes.ts marks all seven endpoints noAuth. The authentication hook in src/app.ts returns before normal session checking for that configuration. registerMobileOperationsRoutes is called by the app's extended-feature registration block; successful registration in a particular deployed instance has not been checked here.

    The acknowledge, escalate, assign and note handlers return fixed success messages without calling persistent incident operations. Home initializes a healthy one-branch summary and a zero critical-alert count; exceptions are swallowed. Call-branch has a fallback phone number. These are source-confirmed behavioral gaps; deployed exposure and exploitability were not tested.

    Required implementation:

    1. Require a valid session and tenant/branch authorization for every route and event subscription, using the canonical authorization path.
    2. Load authoritative scoped health and incidents. Return an explicit unavailable state when data cannot be read; derive critical totals from real records.
    3. Persist actions through the existing incident workflow, validate inputs and assignees, handle conflicts and missing incidents, and record the actor.
    4. Return a branch contact only from configured authorized data. Treat a tel: link as opening the dialer, not a delivered phone call.
    5. Back the event stream with scoped real events, reconnect reconciliation and cleanup; the current handler only writes a connected message.
    6. Verify unauthenticated, wrong-tenant, wrong-branch and forbidden-role requests; test persistence after reload and service restart, store failures, audit failures and simultaneous operators. Inspect reverse-proxy access as well.

    An existing MobileOperationsService is not proof that the registered handlers use it correctly. Review its scope, persistence and data sourcing before wiring it.

    Do not repeat already fixed historical findings¶

    The September customer audit is historical. Current notification worker code rejects failed provider results; the default voice provider fails without a configured transport. The recorder collector returns unavailable evidence on probe failure. Retention UI avoids invented dates/days. Fleet loading batches inventory and AI requests; the stage now includes a fleet wall and excludes P4/P5 operational alerts.

    Local regression results are recorded in the accompanying baseline report. Actual provider, camera, recording and browser behavior still require acceptance.

    AI deployment evidence needs a fresh check¶

    The 8 October presentation report documented stale branch collectors and degraded fire/smoke analytics at that time. It also distinguished training-frame replays from new live validation. Those observations are historical, not a claim about today's services. Recheck fresh branch frames, collector continuity, model health and real operator delivery before demonstrations. A classifier's confidence score is not a product accuracy measurement.

    Delivery sequence¶

    Time windows below are planning horizons, not estimates of committed completion. Assign actual people and effort after reproducing the baseline. Hardware access, model evaluation, security review and staffing are dependencies.

    Horizon Deliverables Responsible role Exit gate
    First week Reproduce client tasks, pin both versions, correct mobile authentication/action/data gaps, refresh live health evidence, agree scorecard and test environment Product lead, backend/security engineer, QA Scoped mobile regression tests pass; no fabricated success in reviewed paths; baseline captured
    Weeks 2-4 Resolve live/startup/stall and playback/export defects found in the pilot; validate recorder models, WAN recovery, reports and real notification receipts Media/edge engineer, backend engineer, QA/operator Core workflow and failure-injection gates pass on representative real devices
    Weeks 5-8 Simplify core workflows, complete mobile/PWA acceptance, validate approval/ticket workflows, evaluate three priority AI use cases Frontend/mobile engineer, ML engineer, product/operator Operator comparison and held-out AI targets pass; mobile changes persist and recover
    Weeks 9-12 Scale and soak testing, restore/failover drills, security review, cost/support package and customer comparison demo Platform/security engineer, QA, commercial/support lead Defined capacity tier passes; risks disclosed; customer accepts measured results

    Reuse current modules before adding new pages or parallel services. Keep a single authoritative incident, notification, health and recording workflow. Fix causes demonstrated by evidence rather than adding counters or success labels that hide failures.

    Fair customer comparison and scoring¶

    Run both products against the same authorized cameras, recording periods, operators, workstation and network constraints. Capture license tier, product version and enabled add-ons. Use identical labelled scenarios for AI. If competitor access is unavailable, mark its outcome unknown; do not declare a win from a website checklist.

    Category Initial weight Sentinel result VC Tech result
    Recording integrity and outage recovery 20% Pending real pilot Unknown
    Live monitoring and performance 15% Pending real pilot Unknown
    Playback, investigation and export 15% Pending real pilot Unknown
    Health and alert reliability 15% Pending real pilot Unknown
    Access control and audit 10% Source gaps identified; acceptance pending Unknown
    AI quality for selected use cases 10% Pending held-out evaluation Unknown
    Operator and mobile usability 5% Acceptance pending Unknown
    Device onboarding and compatibility 5% Acceptance pending Unknown
    Reporting, support and total cost 5% Acceptance pending Unknown

    Weights sum to 100% and should be agreed with the customer. Score each measured category 0-5: 0 fails the required workflow, 1 requires major workarounds, 2 is partial, 3 meets the agreed requirement, 4 exceeds it in a measured way, 5 is a reproducible material improvement accepted by operators. An unknown result is not zero and must not be included in a claimed overall win. Weighted score is sum(weight_percent * score / 5).

    Regardless of total score, unauthorized access, falsely completed incident actions, unreported missing footage and silently lost critical alerts block acceptance. Aim first to pass every required category, then outperform on the customer's highest-value tasks. An overall win does not prove superiority in every individual category.

    Compare three-year cost at the same branch/camera counts, retention policy and service levels: licenses, AI, infrastructure, storage/egress, deployment, maintenance, operator staffing and support. No competitor price advantage is claimed because no quote was provided.

    Evidence to retain for every release¶

    Record test ID, exact revision/artifact, operator, device model/firmware, workload, configuration, expected result, actual result, failure count and artifact link. Keep redacted logs, fresh-frame timestamps, recording checksums, permission results, real provider receipts and operator task measurements. Track open defects with owner, priority, acceptance criterion and rollback plan.

    The first useful customer demonstration is a completed loop: induce a real camera fault, identify it with its timestamp, create a ticket, restore service, confirm recovery, retrieve an incident's footage and export verifiable evidence. Show both successful and unavailable states. Make a stronger product claim only after the same workflow is measured against the client's VMS.
